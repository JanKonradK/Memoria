# Memoria design

## Direction

**Thesis:** A quiet, flat workspace for daily game progress and upcoming events. The games bring the colour; the app brings order and time.

**Story:** Find the game that needs attention, update energy, tick tasks, then check what ends soon.

**Material:** A graphite ground (an off-white one in light mode), hairline rules, no cards around content. Sections are told apart by spacing and a small label over a hairline. Rows have no fill at rest, a quiet fill under a hovering pointer, and a pressed fill with an inset edge when chosen. Floating things (sheets, menus, popovers) are the only raised surfaces.

**Colour:** A game's two logo colours identify it: its logo, its title, its energy meter and its timeline lane. Amber and red are reserved for deadlines and always come with their number. Gold marks a pinned event. The accent marks the selected route, focus, text selection, links and the one primary action; nothing else carries colour. Players choose a colour palette (Memoria, Catppuccin, Kanagawa, Rosé Pine, Gruvbox, Tokyo Night) and an accent within it in Settings → Appearance; every palette is derived in `palettes.ts`, which lifts any colour below AA on its own surfaces.

**Type:** DM Sans for language and numbers, tabular figures wherever a value can change (`.numeral`). Secondary detail may be light italic (`.aside`), but never a time, a date or a count: those are what the product exists to show. A game's own title face is used only for the heading of its page; lists and the timeline use DM Sans in the game's colour.

## Tokens

Use the semantic tokens in `src/index.css`; no raw Tailwind palette colours. The JS mirrors in `theme.ts`, `ui-store.ts` and the pre-paint script in `index.html` must agree with CSS. The theme follows the device unless one is chosen in **Settings → Appearance** (which also holds looks, icon sets, game icons, mark colours/shading/tiles, app backdrops, **Preview Store locks** and detail level; Vibration lives in **Settings → Appearance** and Home timezone in **Settings → Time & reminders**). Memoria's palette is the stylesheet; other palettes are written onto the root element, and the pre-paint script repeats the last one from `memoria-paint` so a reload never flashes.

- Desktop body 16px, metadata 14px, labels and captions 13px. Phone body 17px, metadata 15px, labels 14px, captions 13px. Keep text scaling enabled.
- Text contrast meets WCAG AA. Decorative colour never carries body text.
- Density follows the pointer, not the width: a finger gets 44px targets on a screen of any size (a tablet is not a small PC); only a mouse gets compact controls, through the `fine:` variant and `(pointer: fine)` queries.
- A very wide window keeps pages at a readable 1760px, centred beside the rail; the Calendar keeps every pixel.

## Layout

**The rail** is the app's one navigation, in one order everywhere: **Calendar · Games · Today · Notes · Settings**. Its edge follows the screen's shape. On a wide screen, where height is scarce, it is a column down the left: the places, then every game in the player's own order (Games folds this list away; it is open by default and the choice is remembered), then Settings at the foot. On a tall screen (a phone, an upright tablet) it is a dock **floating** over the bottom edge in every look: five equal places, Today in the middle. Games raises the shelf of game logos out of the dock's top, as one object with it, for a moment: only Games opens it, and picking a game, a tap elsewhere, Escape or a scroll lowers it. On a game's page the dock's Games wears that game's coin. Games carries a rose dot when a game needs the player within two hours. Each game shows its logo over two hairlines (its stamina bar in the game's colour, the bar's bank in its second) and how long until it next needs the player (rose under two hours, amber under eight). The games never re-sort, so a game is always where the player left it. The open game is marked by a pin in its own colour. Ctrl+1 to Ctrl+9 open the first nine games; invariant Alt+1 to Alt+5 open the five places (Calendar, Games, Today, Notes, Settings), and `?` opens the keyboard shortcuts sheet. Only a game's page swipes sideways, through its own tabs; at either end it stretches and stays, and the places never swipe. A drag that starts within 24px of the screen's edge is left to the system's back gesture. Today carries the number of steps left. Sync trouble and a Refresh retry appear down the side only when something failed; upright, the dock keeps its five equal places and trouble is a danger dot on Settings, which then opens Sync. A save error's banner carries its own Refresh data; a waiting update is a dot on Settings, and only a new major or minor version opens the full-screen update page. Under 44px a game's coin shows its short name (ZZZ, NTE, WuWa) zoomed to fill it, never a single letter.

There is no app-wide bar. **Each page has its own bar**: what the page is, its views, and its actions. Adding starts where the thing lives: a game from the rail, an event from the Calendar's bar or a game's Events tab, a reminder from Settings → Time & reminders.

**Today** opens by default and shows Steps or Games. Steps is one ordered to-do list across games: a strip of the next 24 hours (now, quiet hours, a logo at each cap, reset and event end), then the steps grouped by when they are due: within the hour, this evening before quiet hours, before each reset, and this week, followed by non-urgent resource bars (`Other bars`). Each step is done in place: its energy value (with `Spent all` / `Full` and `A/S/D/F` stepping; `Enter` / `Shift+Enter` moves to the next input while `"Order paused · Re-sort now"` holds card order steady), its tick (with Undo), or its count. An energy step carries the bar's bank under its value as a thin meter and figure. Many events of one game ending together are one step. Finished steps fold into Done today. The rest of the week, livestreams, what starts tomorrow and codes sit beside the list once the steps have room for two columns (an upright tablet included), and the list centres on a very wide window. On a phone a focused reading opens a tray under its row (±10, ±1, then Spent all and Full) instead of pushing the row sideways. Games is the ranked list of games, each row two lines (the game and its countdown, then its energy meter); a wide window lays them out as a grid of cards. A game opens its own page at every width, without nesting interactive buttons inside row links.

**A game's page** has the game's coin, name, server and reset in its bar, its tabs (Track, Events, Codes, Pulls, Quests, News, Notes), then Play and Edit. Below 1100px the tabs take their own row and stay pinned while the game's name scrolls away above them; an overflowing tab row fades at the end that scrolls. Track shows energy, tasks (Daily, Cycle, Weekly, Monthly), and the game's events with its notes, in two columns on wide PC screens and three on an ultrawide one. Events manages every event of the game. Codes holds calendar and saved gift codes with one-tap HoYoLAB claiming when linked. Pulls holds savings, pity (with UIGF v4 / WuWa JSON import and read-only PC history scan) and version history (and savings for custom games too); Quests its story progress; News its version notes and livestreams; Notes its game-filtered notes. A tab shows only when the game has something for it. The coin flips between its faces, and its ring turns with the flip, never on its own. Browser Back and Alt+Left return to where the game was opened from.

**A stamina bar and its bank are one instrument.** The bank is whatever holds what the bar cannot: its own reserve (Reserved Trailblaze Power, Backup Battery Charge, Waveplate Crystals, Photo Paper Supply) or a counter crafted from it, paired by name (Condensed Resin with Original Resin). On a game's page the bank is the bar's second row, the same name, stepper, tube and verdict one size down, hung from the bar on a rule; a paired counter is not shown again on its own. Today's step and the rail show it read-only.

**The Calendar** shows the same events four ways: Timeline, Agenda, Month and Ending soon, under one bar with the game filter (a logo per game), the range (Week, 4 weeks, Season), Today, Show finished and Event. The Timeline packs events that do not overlap into shared rows, banners in their own, with a now line, the countdown inside each bar, and a tick on hover or focus. Maintenance is a hatched band, a livestream a diamond, a predicted window a dashed outline; bars are opaque, so the now line never shows through a name. A busy game folds to one line and a miniature of its events. On a narrow window or an upright touch screen the Calendar stacks each game's name above its lanes and folds its tools behind the bar; there a lane opens by itself only for something unfinished ending within two days, and a sideways drag pulls the days with the finger before stepping the week. A phone opens the Calendar on its Agenda. The Agenda groups events by day; the Month grid packs bars per week with a panel for the chosen day; Ending soon puts unfinished events in columns by when they end. New feed events go straight onto the calendar; a failed download offers a retry, and a game whose calendar is behind says so on its lane. Choosing an event opens its quick view, not the editor. A month cell's "+N more" counts only hidden events that start or end that day; one running straight through is in the day's panel. Maintenance labels read away from the now line, which runs under the bars.

**Settings** has a menu beside one section from 900px; below that it is a menu, then one section per page with a back arrow, like a phone's own settings. Game edits save as they are made.

Values can be entered by hand, read opt-in from HoYoLAB or SKPORT (for linked accounts on Windows/Android), or captured locally from single or multi-region screenshots (`Bars & reserves`, `Tasks & dailies`, `Endgame cycles` and `Savings` confirmed together before saving). Each reading states its source and age ("Read from HoYoLAB · 4m ago", "Read from SKPORT · 4m ago", "Read from a screenshot · 12m ago", "Updated 13m ago", or "Starting estimate").

First-run setup picks games, server regions and reminders in three focused steps, then opens the **Guide**: five posture-aware miniature cards (Today, Bar & reserve, Rail or Dock, Calendar & pulls, An overnight end) adapted to phone or desktop. **Settings** opens it again. Guidance stays quiet and in context (key hints like `A/S/D/F`, pause/sort pills, and clear validation errors) instead of modal clutter.

**Words.** A label is the thing and its figures, never an instruction: "Original Resin 137/200", not "Spend Original Resin". Nothing repeats what a figure beside it already says. **An end in the small hours belongs to the night before**: before the player's morning (when their quiet hours end, else 09:00) it is listed on the previous day, its bar stops at that midnight, and a crescent marks its real time. Today asks for it by midnight.

## Looks

A palette is colour; a **look** is form: faces, corners, rules, meters, glow and the dock. Settings → Appearance offers eight, each shown as a working miniature in the player's own palette and games: **Classic** (this document's quiet graphite), **Instrument** (monochrome readout, Doto figures, mono uppercase labels, one red live dot), **Soft HUD** (dot-grid ground, segmented meters, glow only on what is live), **Clean** (flat tonal fills, no lines, heavy type, coins filled in the game's colour), **Glass** (frosted panels over a still glow of the first two games' colours), **Arcade** (stepped corners, blocky meters, pixel figures, octagon coins), **Paper** (serif headings, ink rules, the current place underlined) and **Neon** (game colours as neon outlines over a still horizon). A look never names a colour, so every look works under every palette, theme and accent.

A look is `data-look` on `<html>` (set before first paint, like the palette), and each look's rules sit in an `@scope` block in `src/looks/looks.css` bounded at any nested `[data-look]`, which is how a preview card of one look renders correctly inside an app wearing another. Every `[data-look]` element first takes Classic's token values, then its own look's. A look's faces load only when it is used or previewed. Switching looks cross-fades where the browser can, instantly under reduced motion.

**Detail.** Elaborate shows the words; **Minimal** shows the facts (values, names, times, counts) and the controls, nothing that explains them. Explanatory text carries the `say` class (setting hints, page subtitles, help paragraphs); Minimal clips it to the accessibility tree rather than removing it, and the dock and rail become icons with their names still read. Errors, warnings and permission prompts always keep their words.

## Motion

Motion explains a change of state; it is never decoration. Keep the coin flip, the tick, the strike-through, numbers counting to a new value, rows gliding to a new place, and the logo travelling between a row and its page. Nothing loops while idle except the small breathing dot on an urgent game, which pauses off screen, in hidden tabs and under reduced motion. Reduced motion changes the view immediately. Route content is usable at once.

One scale, in `index.css` and mirrored in `motion.ts`: `--dur-1` 120 ms (a press, a hover), `--dur-2` 180 ms (a small reveal), `--dur-3` 240 ms (a page or panel arriving), `--dur-4` 320 ms (the longest move: the logo morph, a meter filling); `--ease-enter` for arrivals, `--ease-move` for something already on screen changing place, `--ease-exit` for leaving. `--dur-fast`, `--dur-base`, `--dur-slow` and `--ease-out` are aliases on it. Only the phone's drag sheet uses a spring. Animate opacity and transform only; a size, a margin or a grid track never animates per frame (the dock's rising shelf is the one exception, and the dock contains its layout and paint). A page's bar stays still when its place changes: only what is under it arrives, a fade down a wide screen's rail and a 12 px glide from the place's side on the dock (`page-arrival.ts`). Each view transition has a type (`game-logo`, `look`) and its CSS is scoped to it, so the logo morph and a look's cross-fade never borrow each other's rules. One sheet handing over to another keeps its scrim up.

The one piece of motion that is not a change of state is the intro, and it earns its place by covering the load. It lives in `index.html` and paints before any script: one still frame of the ground (#0c0d0f, the same as Android's launch screen and the PC window, so the hand-off is seamless), then the mark fades in as a ghost, lights up left to right, catches one glint and settles in a warm glow (1.4 s on the first visit on a device; on a repeat open it only covers the first paint, with no beat of its own). The app loads and renders underneath. `splash.ts` lifts it once fonts, the active look (`activeLookReady()`), and the first real screen have drawn and the intro has played: `#splash.leave` stops blocking pointer events (`pointer-events: none`), the mark leans in, and the ground lifts off the finished page. If the app needs longer, the glow breathes until it is ready, and gives up at 9 s. A tap skips the rest once the app is ready. It animates only opacity and transform, on layers promoted from the first frame, so the compositor keeps it at full frame rate however busy the page is. Under reduced motion it is a short fade. Automated browsers skip it.

## Validation

Run `npm run check` and the relevant Playwright specs. `npm -w app run shots` draws every page, sheet and state at phone, small phone, phone on its side, the owner's tablet (1095×1750) upright and on its side, a small tablet, PC, 1440p and light mode into `app/build/shots`, with a findings report (overflow, small touch targets, clipped text); look at all of them before calling a layout done. A design change does not change storage or sync.
